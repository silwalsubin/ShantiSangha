# Product Guardian Evaluation: Weekly Summary Email

## Verdict: NO

A weekly summary email showing users their stats from the past week does not belong in ShantiSangha. Here is exactly why.

---

## The Three Questions

### 1. Does it help the user build discipline?

No. It actively works against it. Discipline means showing up **every day**. A weekly summary encourages the user to think about their practice in weekly chunks instead of daily ones. It sends the message: "you can check in once a week and still feel informed." That is the opposite of what this app exists to do.

The daily reflection already serves as the reason to open the app each morning. A weekly email that summarizes what happened creates a substitute for that daily return — the user gets their "progress hit" from the email without ever opening the app.

### 2. Does it give the user clarity on their progress?

Superficially yes, but the Journey tab already does this — and does it better, because it is available in context, at the moment the user is reflecting on their growth. A weekly email delivers progress information at an arbitrary time (when the email lands in their inbox), disconnected from the practice itself. Clarity on progress matters most when the user is *in* the app, grounding themselves. Not when they are scrolling through Gmail.

### 3. Does it contribute to the user's peace of mind?

No. Weekly stat emails create anxiety, not peace. A week where the user checked in 3 of 7 days becomes a report card. Even with warm language, the numbers speak: "you missed 4 days." This is exactly the kind of guilt-driven engagement the app rejects. The user opens their inbox and gets judged by their own app.

---

## Red Flags Triggered

- **"Other apps have this"** — Weekly digest emails are a standard SaaS retention play. That is not a reason for ShantiSangha to adopt them.
- **Features that are useful once but not daily** — A weekly email is, by definition, not a daily practice tool. It is periodic summary content.
- **Dashboards/analytics that require interpretation** — A stats summary is a mini-dashboard delivered via email. The user has to interpret numbers: "is 5 out of 7 good? Was last week better?"
- **Notification philosophy violation** — Would the user thank you for this interruption? A weekly stats email in a cluttered inbox is noise, not value. It fails the notification test from the skill's Notification & Interruption Philosophy.

---

## The Duplication Filter

The Journey tab already exists to show the user how they have grown. Streaks, consistency patterns, insights over time — this is literally what Journey does. A weekly email is a worse version of Journey, delivered in a worse context (email inbox vs. the sacred space of the app).

If Journey is not compelling enough to show weekly progress, the answer is to **deepen Journey**, not to build a separate delivery channel.

---

## What to Do Instead

The instinct behind this proposal is good: you want the user to reflect on their week and feel the weight of their progress. Here are better ways to serve that need, ranked by impact:

### 1. Weekly reflection on the Home tab (High impact, moderate effort)
On the first open of a new week (Monday morning), the daily reflection could naturally incorporate a weekly perspective: "You showed up 6 of 7 days last week. Your longest streak is now 21 days." This is personal, contextual, and happens *inside* the daily practice — not outside it in an email. It strengthens the Mirror hook and gives the user a reason to open the app on Monday specifically.

### 2. Deepen the Journey tab with weekly rhythm (High impact, moderate effort)
Add a subtle weekly heartbeat to Journey — when the user visits, if it is been a full week since they last checked Journey, surface a brief "this week" summary at the top before the longer-term view. This keeps progress visible without creating a new channel or breaking the flow.

### 3. Enhance the daily reflection with trailing-week context (Moderate impact, low effort)
The daily reflection AI already draws from goals, streaks, and patterns. Give it access to a 7-day rolling summary so it can weave weekly observations into the daily mirror naturally: "You have journaled three times this week — that is more than any week before." No new feature. No new screen. Just a smarter reflection.

All three of these strengthen the daily return loop instead of creating an external channel that competes with it. They keep the user inside the sacred space of the app, where the experience is designed and controlled, instead of dumping stats into an inbox where they sit next to marketing spam and work emails.

---

## The Core Problem with Email as a Channel

ShantiSangha is a sacred private space. The moment you start sending emails, you become another app competing for attention in the inbox. You lose control of the experience — you cannot control the font, the context, the mood the user is in when they read it. The app's entire design philosophy is about a controlled, peaceful, intentional environment. Email is the opposite of that.

Build the weekly awareness *into* the daily practice. That is where it belongs.
