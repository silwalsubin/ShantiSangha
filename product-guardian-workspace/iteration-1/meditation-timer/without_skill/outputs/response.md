## Meditation Timer with Guided Sessions -- Product Assessment

### Short answer: No. Not right now, and probably not in this form.

Here is why.

---

### 1. It breaks the simplicity contract

ShantiSangha has a strict rule: 3 tabs, no exceptions. Home, Reflect, Journey. A meditation timer is a standalone activity mode -- it needs its own screen with a countdown, audio controls, session selection, and a "done" state. That is a fourth experience bolted onto an app designed around three. Even if you nest it inside the Home tab, it introduces a fundamentally different interaction pattern (a full-screen timer the user stares at for 10-20 minutes) that does not flow into the existing cycle of ground-reflect-grow.

### 2. It duplicates what goals already solve

Users can already create a recurring daily practice called "Meditate" (or "Sit quietly for 10 minutes") and check it in. The streak mechanic provides accountability. The daily reflection notices when they are consistent. The system already rewards the discipline of meditation without needing to own the meditation session itself.

Adding a timer means ShantiSangha now competes with Insight Timer, Headspace, Calm, and every other meditation app -- all of which have thousands of guided sessions, teachers, and music libraries. You will never win that battle, and trying dilutes what makes this app different.

### 3. Guided sessions are a content treadmill

"Guided sessions" means you need audio content -- recordings, scripts, or AI-generated audio. That is a content production burden that never ends. Users will expect variety, new sessions, different lengths, different themes. This is a product-within-a-product. It pulls engineering, design, and content effort away from the core loop that actually drives retention: the daily reflection, the streaks, the AI companion that knows you.

### 4. It does not strengthen the daily practice circle

The current flow is: Open app -> read your reflection -> check in on practices -> reflect (chat/journal/voice) -> see growth over time. Every feature feeds the next. A meditation timer sits outside this circle. After a 15-minute guided session, what happens? The user closes the app. There is no natural bridge from "I just meditated" to "let me journal about it" unless you engineer one, and even then it feels forced.

---

### What to do instead

If users are asking for meditation support, the real need is probably one of these:

**Option A: Do nothing.** Let users track meditation as a goal with streaks. The app's job is not to be the meditation tool -- it is to be the mirror that notices the user's practice patterns and reflects them back. "You have sat every morning for 12 days. The entries you write after sitting tend to be calmer." That is more powerful than a timer.

**Option B: Simple silent timer (someday, maybe).** If you must build something, the absolute minimum version is a plain countdown timer with no guided audio -- just a circle that counts down and a gentle chime. No session library, no content. It could live as a check-in action on a meditation goal: tap "Start" on the practice card, a timer overlay appears, and when it ends it auto-completes the check-in. This keeps it inside the existing goal system rather than creating a parallel feature. But even this should wait until the core loop is fully proven.

---

### Bottom line

ShantiSangha is not a meditation app. It is a spiritual practice mirror -- it watches how you show up, reflects what it sees, and helps you notice your own growth. A meditation timer pulls it toward being a utility app, which is a crowded market you do not want to enter. Keep the focus narrow. The app's power is in knowing the user, not in running their sessions.
